\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Summary}

This handbook documents the \textbf{Wu Fortran piecewise PV inversion
pipeline} \citep{Davis1991} applied to the 2025-01-08 California
blocking event.  The pipeline is a single, unified method:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
  \item \textbf{Download ERA5} --- single-hour event state + 30-day
        symmetric window for a running-mean climatology
        (steps~01--03).
  \item \textbf{Pass A/B} --- \texttt{pvpialln} computes Ertel-like PV
        and the balanced streamfunction from relative-vorticity
        inversion, for both the mean state and the event state
        (step~05).
  \item \textbf{Pass C (BALNC)} --- \texttt{qinvert21} refines the
        event state to full nonlinear balance
        (\Cref{chap:wu_solver}, step~06).
  \item \textbf{Pass D (BALP)} --- \texttt{qinvertp21} partitions the
        PV perturbation $q' = q_{\text{event}} - q_{\text{mean}}$ into
        three vertical pieces and inverts each to its perturbation
        streamfunction $\psi'$ (step~07).
  \item \textbf{Diagnostics} --- piecewise induced winds,
        PV advection, Fig.~8 cross-comparison, and
        latitude--pressure cross-sections of $V_{\text{induced}}$
        (steps~08--11).
\end{enumerate}

The Fortran core (\texttt{pvpialln}, \texttt{qinvert21},
\texttt{qinvertp21}) is the canonical benchmark; the pipeline's
Python layer (\texttt{wu/steps/}) handles data preparation,
orchestration, post-processing, and visualisation.

\section{Key Finding: Wu PV to SI Conversion is Exactly $10^{-8}$}

A central result of this work (\Cref{chap:wu_coeffs}) is that the Wu
Fortran PV scalar $Q_{\text{Wu}}$ converts to true SI Ertel PV by a
\emph{single universal constant}:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\mathrm{PV}_{\text{SI}}\;[\text{K\,m}^{2}\,\text{kg}^{-1}\,\text{s}^{-1}]
       = Q_{\text{Wu}} \times 10^{-8}\;}
\end{equation}
This holds at \emph{every pressure level} because the Exner-pressure
scaling $\Pi^{-2.5}$ in the Wu PV definition is designed such that the
conversion factor's pressure dependence cancels exactly when
$\kappa = 2/7$.  The earlier practice of dividing by a
spatially-varying median ratio was incorrect --- the residual
$\sim$3.5$\times$ difference between Wu-Q$\times10^{-8}$ and ERA5
native PV reflects the physical discretisation bias of the coarse
$1.5^{\circ}$ finite-difference grid, not a missing conversion factor.

\section{Status}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
  \item \textbf{Pass A/B} --- compute and converge reliably.
  \item \textbf{Pass C (BALNC)} --- converges for this case; the
        total-balance refinement confirms the \texttt{pvpialln} state
        is already balanced to solver tolerance.
  \item \textbf{Pass D (BALP)} --- three-piece perturbation inversion
        converges; Piece~3 (upper troposphere, 600--250\,hPa) carries
        the dominant blocking signal.
  \item \textbf{All output NetCDFs} are in SI units
        (\Cref{tab:si_units}); the pipeline is self-consistent.
  \item \textbf{The Fig.~8 diagnostic} reproduces the expected
        $\Sigma$/Direct RMS ratio ($\sim$1.12 for the California
        window).
\end{itemize}

\section{Non-Dimensional Scales (Reference)}

The Wu Fortran BALNC/BALP solvers use the following non-dimensional
scales ($N_L = 8$ levels in our configuration):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Scale & Definition & Value ($N_L{=}8$) \\
\midrule
DPI     & $500/N_L$           & $62.5$ \\
LL      & $(2{\times}10^{7}/\pi)\cdot\Delta\lambda\cdot\pi/180$ & $1.667{\times}10^{5}$\,m \\
FF      & reference Coriolis  & $10^{-4}$\,s$^{-1}$ \\
THO     & $\mathrm{FF}^{2}\,\mathrm{LL}^{2}/\mathrm{DPI}$ & $4.445$\,K \\
HND     & $\mathrm{DPI}\cdot\mathrm{THO}/g$ & $28.32$\,m \\
QCONST  & $10^{6}\kappa g c_p\,\mathrm{FF}\,\mathrm{THO}/(p_0\,\mathrm{DPI})$ & $0.313$\,PVU-equiv \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

These are documented in \texttt{config.py} for reference; they are
\emph{not} used by the Python post-processing layer, which works
entirely in SI.
