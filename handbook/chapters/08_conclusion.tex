\chapter{Conclusions and Outlook}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Summary}

This handbook documents two tracks for piecewise PV inversion of the
2025-01-08 California blocking event:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item the \textbf{Wu Fortran reference} (Part~I,
        \Cref{chap:wu_solver,chap:wu_pipeline}), a finite-difference SOR
        solver that runs end-to-end and reproduces the Davis 2022
        Fig.~8 diagnostic.  It is the project \emph{benchmark};
  \item the \textbf{Wu Python finite-difference port} (Part~II,
        \Cref{chap:fd,chap:wupy_pv,chap:wu_balnc}), a pure-Python
        re-implementation of Wu's non-dimensional balance system on the
        same regional $51\times87$ grid, using finite differences with
        numba-accelerated SOR and implicit tridiagonal vertical coupling.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Both tracks share the mathematical foundations of
\Cref{chap:math} (Ertel PV, hydrostatic and balance closures,
linearization) and the Wu non-dimensionalization of \Cref{chap:wu_balnc}.

\section{Status and Known Limitations}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Wu Fortran reference --- working.}  Pass~A/B/C/D complete and
        the Fig.~8 replica (\Cref{fig:wu_fig8}) reproduces the
        published structure.  For this case the total-balance Pass~C is
        a consistency check: it leaves the \texttt{pvpialln} field
        unchanged to solver tolerance.
  \item \textbf{Wu Python port --- working, 90$\times$ faster than serial.}
        The full pipeline (steps 05--10) runs end-to-end in $\sim$30\,s on
        384 cores.  The BALNC solver uses a parallel implicit tridiagonal
        H-solver (Thomas algorithm per column, \texttt{prange} over columns)
        and parallel red-black SOR for the $\psi$ equation.  Piecewise
        Pass~D uses \texttt{ProcessPoolExecutor} for 3-piece parallelism.
        The $\psi$ field matches the Fortran reference to $\sim$5\% (p99
        relative error).
  \item \textbf{Piecewise perturbation amplitudes.}  The nonlinear piecewise
        approach (BALNC per piece) yields $\psi' \sim 10^{7}$--$10^{8}$\,m$^2$/s,
        dominated by the mean-state response.  The linear BALP port
        (\texttt{piecewise.py}) gives $\psi' \sim 200$--$400$\,m$^2$/s for
        this case --- algorithmically correct but underestimates the
        perturbation because the linearization around the mean state does
        not capture the nonlinear coupling.
  \item \textbf{$f_0$-plane closure.}  The legacy linear closure
        $\dPhi\approx f_0\dpsi$ (\Cref{sec:closure}) under-estimates
        upper-level geopotential and accounts for part of the residual
        discrepancy between the Wu Python and Wu Fortran $\psi$ fields.
        for an $\approx\!0.1\times$ amplitude bias relative to the Wu
        reference.
\end{itemize}

\section{Next Steps}

\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Converge BALNC (Pass~C) in the SH port.}  Candidate
        fixes (\Cref{sec:wu_open}): solve the $H$--$\psi$ system
        \emph{coupled} rather than alternating; treat the vertical
        $BB/BH/BL$ coupling \emph{implicitly} (tridiagonal in $k$);
        relax the first $H$ update.
  \item \textbf{Run the SH BALP pass} (\texttt{balp.py}) once Pass~C is
        stable, inlining the same $n{\le}1$ mode projection.
  \item \textbf{Quantitative validation} of the SH port against the Wu
        Fortran benchmark (\Cref{fig:wu_fig8}), once it converges.
\end{enumerate}
